# Persia

**Repair: orient. The distinctive move is what the root declines to know.**

## The containers

**Container.** A satrapy. Prompts are *tribute*, *levy*, *keep the peace*.
Responses are silver, soldiers, and the absence of revolt.

**The response type is deliberately thin.** Nothing in it describes how the silver
was raised, which law was applied, or which god was worshipped. That thinness is
the abstraction barrier, and it is the entire design.

**Composition.** $\boxtimes$ across satrapies. They run in parallel and do not
consume each other's interfaces, so no cycle ever forms and the deadlock question
never arises. The empire is a star and the king is the root.

**Contrast with Gosplan.** Same combinator, same topology, opposite discipline.
Gosplan's prompts reach into the implementation and its response type tries to
carry the interior back. Persia asks for a value at the boundary and refuses to
look behind it.

The Achaemenid empire ran twenty-odd satrapies for two hundred and twenty years.
Darius fixed the annual tribute per satrapy in advance, in silver, and Herodotus
records the schedule. The tribute was predictable rather than arbitrary, and that
single fact is what separates the arrangement from a racket.

Tilly's argument is that states and racketeers both sell protection against a
danger they largely manufacture, and that legitimacy is granted retrospectively by
success and duration. The distinction that rescues Persia is Olson's. A roving
bandit takes everything and moves on. A stationary bandit knows he will be back
next year, so he takes a sustainable share and acquires a real interest in the
productivity of his subjects. A long time horizon converts predation into
governance. A racket does not build roads, and Persia built the royal road.

## Why it belongs in these notes

The satrapy system is a star, exactly like Gosplan. Tribute flows up and
protection flows down. But the centre made no attempt to plan the interior. It
specified an interface, being this much silver, these troops, and no rebellion,
and left implementation entirely to the satrap. Local law, local cult and local
elites were untouched.

That is an abstraction barrier. Gosplan tried to know everything and drowned in
reports that terror had already made worthless. Persia deliberately declined to
know, and asked only for a well typed response at the boundary.

Same topology, opposite failure mode. The difference is whether the root specifies
the implementation or only the interface.

## The audit was built in

Satraps governed, but garrison commanders and treasury officials frequently
reported to the king on separate lines, and royal inspectors travelled. Two
channels which ought to agree is precisely the cross check, and it is Neil's
question about assuring delegated work answered in the sixth century BC.

## The cost of the design

An abstraction barrier makes the implementation swappable. Alexander took the
empire largely by leaving the satrapies intact and replacing only the root, and
the satrapies changed allegiance without changing behaviour, because from below
nothing distinguished the new king from the old one.

That is the trade in full. Specify only the interface and you gain two centuries
of stability and lose the ability to prevent your own substitution.
