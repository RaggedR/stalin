# Mergers and acquisitions

**Repair: contested, and the contest is the whole subject. A merger of equals is
the confederation claim, and it usually fails for the reason confederations fail.**

## The containers

**Container.** A firm. Prompts come from customers, suppliers and capital.
Responses are products, deliveries and cash.

**A merger of equals is a composite with two roots, which is no root.** The prompts
that require a single answer, meaning which system, which process, which person,
have two well typed responses and no rule for choosing. The composite is not a
function.

**Berkshire is Persia.** Subsidiaries composed by $\boxtimes$, an interface of
*send the cash and do not lose money*, and a response type carrying nothing about
the interior. Twenty five people at the root over several hundred thousand in the
leaves, which is only possible because the root declined to know.

**Earnouts are the guard.** Consideration is deferred and contingent, so the cycle
is forced through a delay and neither party can demand the fixpoint today.

**Antitrust is a root outside the set**, which is the second claim from the index
appearing in commercial form. Firms will not accept a competitor as sovereign, and
they will accept a regulator, because the regulator is not one of them.

**The commutativity test.** Bolt-ons into separate markets commute, so integration
is nearly free. Two salesforces, two ERP systems and two head offices are two
implementations of one interface, and only one can survive.

Two firms with their own interfaces choose to compose. The only question that ever
matters is whether the composite has a root and whose it is.

## The merger of equals is the confederation claim

DaimlerChrysler in 1998 was sold as a merger of equals and was a Daimler
acquisition. Two headquarters, two cultures, and no agreed root. It was unwound in
2007 at enormous cost. The phrase itself is now close to a warning label in the
literature, and the reason is structural rather than cultural. A composite with two
roots has no root, and the decisions that require one simply do not resolve.

## Dual parent structures can last, and they end the same way

Unilever ran as NV and PLC from 1930 until it unified in 2020. Royal Dutch Shell
ran dual parent from 1907 until 2005. Rio Tinto is still dual listed.

Ninety years is not a failure by any standard. But the direction of travel is
consistent, and each of these eventually chose a root or is under pressure to. The
federation is stable for a long time and then it is not, which is the same profile
Ibn Khaldun gives for asabiyya.

Worth noting that Unilever, the reference in the invisibles note, was itself a
federation of two companies for ninety years before it picked a side.

## Integration is choosing the spanning tree

Post merger integration is entirely the question of whose systems, whose
processes, whose reporting lines and whose people. That is orienting the graph,
and everybody involved knows exactly what is at stake, which is why it is the part
that goes wrong.

## The federation that works declines to integrate

Berkshire runs roughly twenty five people at headquarters over a group employing
several hundred thousand. Buffett does not integrate and does not direct. He
specifies an interface, being send the cash to Omaha and do not lose money, and
declines to know the interior.

That is Persia. Same repair, same abstraction barrier, same result, which is
unusual durability bought by giving up the ability to control implementation.

JAB is the same model applied to coffee and casual dining.

## Two smaller mechanisms worth naming

Earnouts and deferred consideration are the guard repair. Payment is contingent
and delayed, so the cycle is forced through a step and the parties cannot demand
resolution today.

Antitrust is a root placed above the federation of firms by something none of them
is, which is the second claim from the index appearing in commercial form.

## The commutativity test predicts the outcome

Bolt on acquisitions into separate markets commute. Order does not matter, nothing
contends, and integration is cheap because there is almost none.

Two overlapping salesforces, two ERP systems and two head offices do not commute.
Whichever is adopted first determines the outcome, the operations contend, and the
merger produces a centre or produces nothing.

Which is the general claim restated in the one domain where somebody computes the
cost afterwards and publishes it.
