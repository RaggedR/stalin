# USA

**Repair: orient, unroll and guard. Three of the four, applied deliberately, in
one document.**

## The containers

**Container.** A state. Prompts are *requisition*, *muster*, *enforce*. Responses
are money, troops, compliance.

**The Articles' defect stated exactly.** The response type of *requisition*
admitted a null. It returned a `Maybe`, and Congress needed a value. No amount of
prompting fixes a response type that is allowed to be empty, which is why eight
years of requisitions produced eight years of nothing.

**What 1787 actually changed.** Not merely supremacy. The federal government
acquired its own interface directly onto the citizen, rather than composing
through the states as intermediaries. Federal law binds people, not member
governments. That removes the state from the response path entirely for the things
that matter, which is why the null disappeared.

**Dred Scott.** The prompt is *what is this person's legal status*. Under a
federation of divergent state law the response depended on the path travelled, so
it was not a function. A response type that is path dependent is not a type, and
the composite had no fixpoint.

The United States ran the experiment properly and wrote down what happened, which
makes it the best available test of the whole argument.

## The Articles were an actual confederation and they deadlocked

Congress could requisition money from the states but could not tax. It sent the
prompt and the response never came back. Amendment required unanimity, so Rhode
Island alone killed the impost of 1781 and New York killed the next one. No
executive, no judiciary, and states levying tariffs against each other.

Prompts propagated freely for eight years and replies did not. That is deadlock in
its pure form, and it is the response direction failing exactly where the variance
argument says it will.

## 1787 applies two repairs crudely and a third elegantly

The Supremacy Clause chooses a root and breaks the cycle by orienting the graph.

Ratification by nine of thirteen rather than thirteen of thirteen is bounded
unrolling. Take the approximation rather than waiting for a fixpoint that will
never arrive.

And then the third, which is the interesting one. Staggered elections, six year
Senate terms with a third turning over each cycle, an amendment process measured
in years, litigation measured in years. Every turn of the feedback loop is forced
to cost a step.

That is guarded recursion. The Articles had a cycle and no delay. The Constitution
has a cycle with a delay on every path through it, which is precisely the
condition under which the fixpoint exists. The delay is not friction in the
design. The delay is what makes it terminate.

## The commutativity prediction, and the war

The federation holds where state operations commute, being licensing, education
and local law, and centralises where they do not, being currency, defence and
interstate commerce.

The extreme case is slavery. Whether a person is free cannot depend on which route
they travelled. Dred Scott is exactly the question of whether status changes when
someone is carried across a boundary, which is an order dependence question. The
operations did not commute, the federation had no way to type it, and the
resolution took six hundred thousand lives.

The war was the system failing to have a fixpoint.

## Present tense, moving both ways at once

Centralised through the income tax, the Commerce Clause after Wickard, and
conditional spending which buys compliance rather than commanding it.

Re-federalising through the anti-commandeering doctrine, cannabis being federally
illegal and openly sold, sanctuary jurisdictions, California setting emissions
standards the whole market then follows, and abortion law diverging completely by
state after Dobbs.

So it is neither of the two figures. It is a rooted composite with a guarded
feedback channel, and the exit was closed by Texas v. White in 1869, which is what
distinguishes it from the EU.
