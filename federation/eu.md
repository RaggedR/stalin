# EU

**Repair: all four, and none of them settled. The only case still openly arguing
about whether it has a root.**

## The containers

**Container.** A member state, with a genuinely thick interface. Unlike an American
state it retains its own foreign policy, its own tax base, and an exit.

**Unanimity is the Articles' defect, restored on purpose.** In the unanimity
domains the composite requires every member's response to be affirmative, so a
single null annihilates the result. Hungary is not exploiting a loophole. It is
returning the value the type permits.

**QMV is the composite accepting a partial response set.** The fixpoint is
approximated rather than awaited, and the holdouts are outvoted rather than waited
for.

**The supremacy contest is two candidate orientations of the same diagram.** The
Court of Justice asserted one root, several national constitutional courts assert
another, and nothing in the treaties adjudicates between them. A diagram with two
proposed roots and no agreement is exactly a federation that has not chosen.

**The euro.** The monetary container was centralised and the fiscal container was
not, and they share state. Two morphisms writing to the same value with no
sequencing between them, which is the plainest possible failure of commutativity.

Every other case here resolved the question one way or the other. The EU is the
live one, which makes it the most useful to watch and the hardest to describe.

## The deadlock is not historical, it is current

Unanimity still governs foreign policy, taxation and enlargement. One member can
stop the federation acting, and one member regularly does. That is the Articles of
Confederation problem running in the present tense, and everybody involved knows
it.

## All four repairs are visible at once

**Unroll.** Qualified majority voting is the approximation taken rather than the
fixpoint awaited. Fifty five per cent of states representing sixty five per cent of
the population, and the holdouts are simply outvoted. It covers most single market
legislation and pointedly not the areas above.

**Orient, contested.** The supremacy of EU law was asserted by the Court in Costa
v ENEL and Van Gend en Loos, not written plainly into the founding treaties. The
Court chose a root. Member state constitutional courts have been contesting it ever
since, through the German Solange line and the PSPP ruling of 2020, and the Polish
Constitutional Tribunal in 2021. A federation whose highest courts disagree about
which of them is highest has not settled whether it has a root.

**Guard.** The EU runs on delay more than any other institution here. Multi year
legislative processes, transition periods, review clauses, and the shadow of a
vote that has not been taken yet. Guarded recursion as an operating culture.

**Commute.** The single market is the commuting part, which is why it succeeded
first and most completely. Standards, mutual recognition and free movement are
operations that do not depend on the order in which members adopt them.

## Where it does not commute

The euro is monetary policy centralised without fiscal policy centralised, which is
a non-commuting operation without a root. The crisis of 2010 to 2012 was the
federation unable to decide, and Draghi saying whatever it takes in 2012 was the
centre acting beyond a mandate nobody had given it because the alternative was
deadlock.

NextGenerationEU in 2020 was the first large common borrowing, and it was widely
called a Hamiltonian moment, which is a direct reference to the assumption of state
debts in 1790. Whether it was a precedent or a one off is the open question, and it
is the same question the Americans answered in 1790 by choosing a root.

## What makes it more confederal than the United States

Article 50. Exit exists and has been used. Texas v. White closed that in America in
1869 and nothing equivalent has happened here.

So the EU is the genuine confederation of the seven, and it pays the genuine price,
which is that it cannot reliably act when a member declines.
