STALIN — game description
=========================

The original design brief, lightly tidied. It arrived in three parts, and
they are kept in that order because the third one is what pulls the first two
together.


I. THE ECONOMY
--------------

Let's build a game using this new technology. Brainstorm first, before you
jump in and start building.

The game is called "stalin". The goal is to build tractors. To do that you
need steel and trains.

The resources are human labour and money. Money is obtained by selling grain
to the USA — but the rural farmers have to eat that grain too.

To make steel you must hire German engineers. To build railways you need
steel. Rural farmers need grain; rural farmers can also work on the trains
and in the steel mill.


II. THE AIM
-----------

The aim is to build the tractors so that the agricultural output of the rural
workforce is greater than it would have been without them — while killing as
few peasants as possible.

Peasants then become tractor drivers, railway workers and steel workers.

You need steel to build railways, and at the beginning you can buy steel
abroad in exchange for grain. Steel is never sold: a country that exports its
steel is not industrialising, and everything the plan is for is made of it.
Grain is the only export, so the whole apparatus rests on taking grain from
people who were going to eat it.

And the railway comes first. Grain the railway cannot carry rots at the
sidings, so a quota set without track to move it is not thrift — it is a
harvest going bad in a field, and the villages went hungry for nothing.


III. THE WAR
------------

Part II of the game is to use the surplus steel to build armies, and defeat
Germany and then the USA. We will implement that later.

Or perhaps there is a time limit. If you have not reached a surplus of steel
after some finite number of turns, you cannot defeat Germany — so you have to
decide whether to invest steel in tractors, in railways, or in the army. You
must reach a minimum quota of surplus steel before a certain date in order to
win. Peasants must also serve as soldiers.

To fight the war you need both steel and soldiers. With less steel you have to
sacrifice more people to win; with more steel you sacrifice fewer. So it is a
trade-off between letting peasants starve — in order to build more railways
and more steel — and letting soldiers die because there was not enough steel
to equip them.

The goal is to kill as few people as possible, and also to win the war.
